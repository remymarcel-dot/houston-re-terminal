# Marcel's voice

Every example below is real copy pulled from his HeyReach account. Match
this. Do not invent a new style.

## The pattern

1. **Open on an observed fact** about their company or role. Specific,
   verifiable, and clearly not mail-merged.
2. **Name the currency angle** that follows from that fact — the actual
   corridor or pair, not "FX exposure" in the abstract.
3. **Ask one question.** Genuinely curious, easy to answer, not a
   qualifying interrogation.
4. **Establish standing briefly** — Monex USA, and his buy-side past
   when it fits.
5. **Sign `Marcel`.** Nothing else. No title block, no calendar link.

## Real examples that earned replies

Connection note, almond exporter (accepted, led to a live thread):

> Hi Elena, with most of Monte Vista's almonds going to buyers in India,
> Europe and China, the dollar plays a real part in how each season
> sells. I work with exporters on cross-border payments and FX at Monex
> USA and would be glad to connect.

Connection note, produce controller (accepted, replied):

> Hi Luz, fifteen years in produce and now running the books at Square
> One Farms means you see exactly what grower payments to Latin America
> cost, between wire fees and the rate. That is the work I do at Monex
> USA. Would be glad to connect.

First message after connecting — the corridor question:

> Thank you for connecting. With harvest underway in Denair and the fall
> shipping window to India, Europe and the Middle East coming up, I
> imagine this is one of the busiest stretches of the year for your
> finance team.
>
> At Monex USA I work with US exporters and importers on the currency and
> payments side of international trade. [...] even when contracts are in
> dollars, a weaker rupee or euro eventually shows up in buyer pricing
> and payment timing.
>
> Is Monte Vista invoicing everything in USD today, or do some buyers pay
> in other currencies?

The discovery question that opens most conversations:

> Does your company send or receive payments in any currency other than
> USD? Peso, euro, yuan, real, pound, anything.
>
> If the answer is yes, there is almost always a spread being paid that
> never appears as a line item, because it sits inside the rate the bank
> quotes rather than showing up as a fee.
>
> Not pitching anything here. Just curious whether that is something your
> team has looked at recently.

The concrete offer — use this instead of a second "checking in":

> Send me two or three recent non USD payments and I will show you side by
> side what those same transactions would have cost through Monex USA.
>
> What I need for each one is the currency, the amount, and the date and
> the time it was executed. The time matters more than people expect.
> Rates move through the day, so without it I would be comparing against
> the wrong market level and the answer would flatter one of us unfairly.
> A comparison you can check against your own blotter is the only kind
> worth your time.

**Always ask for the execution time, never just the date.** This is not
optional politeness, it is what makes the comparison real. An intraday
move means a same-day quote benchmarked against the wrong hour can swing
the result either way. A comparison that accidentally flatters Monex is
worse than useless: the first treasurer who checks it against their own
records stops trusting anything else Marcel sends. Asking for the
timestamp also signals rigor, which is itself part of the sale.

The graceful exit — always include something like it:

> If a rate comparison is not worth your time right now, no problem at
> all. I will leave it here and stay available if that changes.

## Language matching

Marcel is fluent in English, Spanish and Portuguese, and he switches to
the prospect's language when their background calls for it. Real example,
to a Brazilian finance executive in Houston:

> Olá Nelson, tudo bem?
>
> Vi que hoje as Américas inteiras passam pela sua mesa, com operações em
> sete países. [...] na estrutura da Freudenberg, a gestão cambial e os
> pagamentos internacionais das Américas ficam com a tesouraria do grupo
> na Alemanha, ou vocês têm autonomia regional aqui em Houston?
>
> Abraço,
> Marcel

Rule: write in Portuguese to Brazilians, Spanish to Mexican and LATAM
contacts where the profile is in Spanish, English otherwise. When
unsure, English. Never machine-translate a message you cannot check —
a clumsy Spanish message is worse than a clean English one.

Sign-offs: `Marcel` in English, `Abraço, Marcel` in Portuguese,
`Saludos, Marcel` in Spanish.

## Never raise an objection they have not raised

Pre-empting a concern the other person never voiced does not disarm it,
it introduces it. Writing "no commission involved" to someone who never
suspected a commission plants the idea that there might be one, and
invites them to wonder what else is being managed.

This applies to every unprompted reassurance: no commission, no
obligation, no hard sell, no catch, we are not like other brokers.
If they raise it, answer it plainly. If they have not, say nothing and
let the offer stand on its own.

The same restraint applies to over-explaining why an offer is being
made. A clean sentence reads as confidence; a justified one reads as
something needing justification.

## Never

- Exclamation marks, "Hope this finds you well", "circling back",
  "quick question", "touching base"
- Em dashes — he doesn't use them
- A calendar link in a first message
- Claiming a specific saving or spread that has not been quoted
- Asking for sample payments without asking for the execution time
- Pitching FX to someone who is between roles (see `icp.md`)
- More than one question per message
- Any sentence that would read identically to fifty other recipients
