## Condition findings

### Structural — the deal-killer

Movement is showing up in **four independent building systems**, which is what separates this from
ordinary Houston clay noise:

1. **Door frames racked out of square.** Tapered reveals (light gap wider at top than bottom),
   casing mitre joints pulled open at door heads, vertical separation between casing and wall on
   multiple openings. This is the most diagnostic interior sign there is — drywall cracks have many
   innocent causes, an out-of-plumb opening does not.
2. **Masonry fireplace separating from the structure.** Full-height gap where the brick chase meets
   the drywall, plus separation along the ceiling line. Efflorescence across the brick = water is
   moving through the chimney (crown or roof flashing).
3. **Ceiling-to-wall tape separation** in at least two rooms — compound cracked and lifting.
4. **Roof ridge deflection.** The hip ridge is not straight; it dips and humps. Buckled shingle
   courses and a diagonal wrinkle telegraphing through the field.

Flatwork corroborates: driveway cracked across multiple slabs with **vertical displacement** and
spalling at the garage apron; wide open crack in the side-door patio running to the threshold.
Flatwork alone proves nothing (thin, unreinforced, pine roots at the driveway edge) — but it is
consistent.

> One hallway photo looked clean early in the survey. It was not representative.

### Roof — end of life, with a structural question

3-tab (not architectural), heavy granule loss, ~15–18 yr realistic life in Houston heat. Valleys
packed with pine straw holding moisture. Algae streaking. **The deck deformation means tear-off plus
decking replacement, with framing inspected once open.** Layer count unknown — a second layer means
double tear-off and most carriers won't write a two-layer roof at all.

### Envelope

- Hardboard siding **delaminating and rotted at grade** — panel replacement, plus grade clearance correction.
- **Door jambs rotted through** with open holes. Security exposure on a vacant house.
- Fascia/soffit rot and peeling paint at multiple corners; gap opening at a soffit-to-wall joint.
- Alligatored paint on exterior doors — full repaint, side door likely cheaper to replace.
- Ivy growing directly against the wall (cause of much of the rot).

### Site

- Grass knee-high across the lot — deed-restriction / nuisance-citation exposure while vacant.
- **Fence failing, not weathered** — broken and missing boards, a leaning run, snapped brace, vines
  pulling sections down. Replacement on 1–2 runs.
- Pine at the driveway edge driving both root heave and the valley debris.

## Additional risk flags

- **Insurability.** Carriers routinely decline or heavily surcharge a roof of this age with visible
  deck deflection. Price the policy *before* committing, not after.
- **Cause vs. symptom.** In Houston a leaking under-slab sewer line is frequently the *cause* of
  movement. Piering without a hydrostatic test buys a repeat performance.
- **Disclosure.** Check whether prior foundation work exists and whether a transferable warranty
  came with it.

## Recommendation

**PASS.** Both exit paths converge on a purchase price at or below lot value, and that is before the
foundation scope resolves. This is the analog of the house rule "title defects = HIGH RISK regardless
of spread" — active multi-system movement is not a spread problem, it is a scope-unknowable problem.

**Reconsider only if all four hold:**

1. Structural engineer's elevation survey shows movement isolated to the **chimney footing**, not the slab.
2. Hydrostatic test passes clean.
3. Confirmed **Zone X** (not SFHA).
4. Acquisition at **≤ $30K**.

If the engineer instead finds slab tilt, walk and don't look back.
