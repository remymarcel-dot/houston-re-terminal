> Kevin, I said in September I would explain how that gets unpicked, so here it is.
> Nothing needed back.
>
> When a factory quotes you in dollars it has picked a rate to protect itself and built
> a cushion into the unit price. You cannot see it because it is not a line item, it is
> inside the number. Three steps make it visible.
>
> One. Ask the factory to quote the same SKU in RMB alongside the USD price. Convert the
> RMB figure at the day's rate. The gap is the cushion.
>
> Two. Do that across your factories and compare. It varies by supplier, which tells you
> who is pricing the dollar aggressively and who is not.
>
> Three. Once you can see it, it becomes a choice: pay in RMB and carry the risk
> yourself, which is usually cheaper, or leave it with them knowing what it costs.

>

>
> Marcel

**Why this is the strongest note of the seven:** it is a procedure, not an argument. A
controller can run step one on Monday without talking to anybody, and if the gap is
real he has found money by himself, which is a far better start than being told about it.

# What Marcel must supply: nothing

**Marcel's rule, 2026-10-02: "never puts numbers."** Every rate block is removed and all
five remaining notes send as written.

**The reasons the rule is right, recorded so this does not recur:**

- **A written rate becomes a benchmark he is held to**, and can read as an indication or
  an offer rather than a reference point.
- **It goes stale the moment it is sent**, and a figure in someone's budget file outlives
  its own validity.
- **It reduces the exchange to price.** A number invites rate shopping. A structural read
  invites a conversation, which is the entire object.

**Every note still works, and two are better for it.** Kevin's is a procedure he can run
himself, which never needed a figure. Amaya's point is the gap between the parent's rate
and the locally executable one, which is a structural claim, not a level. Katie's
structural read of the four corridors IS the reference point she was promised.

**If anyone comes back asking for levels, that is a reply**, and levels get discussed
live rather than written down.
