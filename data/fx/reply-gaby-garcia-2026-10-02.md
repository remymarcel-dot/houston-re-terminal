She did not have to add the second sentence. **That is an unsolicited reference
from a VP of Finance**, given to a stranger who had just pitched her her own
supplier. It is worth more than the meeting the email was asking for.

**The screen never checked whether she was already a client.** We spent ZoomInfo
credits on her, built an argument around Tecma's peso payroll, and sent it to a
customer who has been happy with Monex for some time.

Checking the client list belongs at the very top of the screen, before
enrichment and before drafting. It is now written into her record.

## What this is actually worth

The account is already won, so the value here is not the account.

**Tecma is a shelter operator with 90+ cross border manufacturing clients, and
its VP of Finance volunteers that Monex has been a very good partner.** That is
the strongest channel position anywhere in this book. Every one of those 90+
manufacturers runs the same dollar-in, peso-out problem.

**Do not reach for that by email.** Not in this reply, not in the next one. It is
an in-person conversation with someone who has just done you a favour.

## One thing to do before 22 October

**Find out internally who owns the Tecma relationship at Monex.** Walking up to a
colleague's client at their own stand, unannounced, is the single way to turn
this into a problem. Ask your own team, not Gaby.

## Notes on the draft

**The apology is one clause, not a paragraph.** "Clearly unnecessary" does the
work with a bit of humour and then gets out of the way. Any more would make her
manage your embarrassment, which is a cost she should not pay for being kind.

**The US entity line is the real content.** She said Monex handles their currency
trade *into* Mexico. Marcel is Monex USA. Whether that is the same house or not,
the US side of Tecma — the El Paso entity that collects client fees in dollars —
is a distinct conversation, and nobody has had it with her.

**"Nothing to act on" is what makes it sendable.** It removes the obligation and
lets the information sit until she needs it, which with a happy client is exactly
how this should work.

**The booth line concedes the obvious.** A CFO is not standing at stand 181 for
two days. Saying so first makes the offer easy to accept and easy to decline,
and shows you understand her job.

**No tenure claim and no Juarez flattery.** She was CFO Mexico in 2015 and 2016,
left to Capgemini, returned in 2018. Nothing in this reply depends on a number
that could be wrong.
