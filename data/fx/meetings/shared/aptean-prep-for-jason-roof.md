### Why it matters

Kantox is Currency Management Automation and has been 100% owned by BNP
Paribas since July 2023. It automates the end to end corporate FX
workflow: capturing exposure data, automating hedging, and running
payment and collection processes with pricing embedded.

Set that beside Madhu's own record: *"led payment automation
initiatives"* and *"supported treasury system implementation."*

So the sharpest question on our list, whether rate capture sits inside
the payment automation or outside it, may already have the answer
"inside, via Kantox."

### Two versions of the call

**If they are not on it.** Proceed as prepped below. The gap is real and
the long tail argument holds.

**If they are on it.** The honest position is not "replace Kantox."
Kantox automates the *workflow*. It does not by itself determine who is
on the other side of the trade, and under BNP ownership execution
gravitates to BNP pricing. So:

> "Kantox handles the workflow well. The part worth checking is who is
> pricing the other side, and whether that is the right counterparty for
> every corridor or just the European ones."

BNP is strong across Europe. It is not automatically the best price in
Mexico, LATAM or parts of APAC. The ask becomes narrower and more
credible: keep the automation, price three corridors independently,
compare.

**One thing to avoid either way:** asking "did you evaluate Kantox?"
directly. It sounds like checking up on them. The question we already
plan to ask, where rate capture sits, gets there on its own, and if they
are on Kantox they will say so themselves.

## The trap in this account

Their strategy is **fewer banks**. Account rationalisation is an active
workstream Madhu owns. A new provider looks, on its face, like the
opposite of that. If we do not address it directly, both of them will be
thinking it and neither will say it.

**The reframe to have ready in one sentence:** the reason they still
keep bank seven, eight, nine and ten is almost certainly the long tail,
the currencies and countries their main banks serve badly. One provider
covering that tail lets rationalisation go **further**, not backwards.
We replace the banks they only keep for awkward corridors rather than
adding to the count.

## What to avoid

- **Asking how many accounts.** Published.
- **Explaining India, INR or payment operations** to a man who ran
  treasury at Ola.
- **Pitching a bank replacement.** Ten banking relationships with SOX
  compliance and pooling structures do not get swapped out.
- **Presenting capabilities in the first half.** The message that won
  this meeting had no product in it.

## The realistic prize

Not the treasury, and not the ten banks. **One corridor, priced.**

The outcome to aim for: *"name the three corridors that annoy you most
and we will price them against what you pay now."* Madhu can authorise
that without a committee, it is measurable, which is how he thinks, and
it does not disturb the rationalisation programme.
