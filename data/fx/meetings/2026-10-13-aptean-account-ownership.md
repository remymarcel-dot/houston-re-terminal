# Aptean account ownership, and the email to Jason Roof

## The email, final

**Subject: Aptean, Oct 13 call and how you want to handle this**

Hi [Name],

I went to register Aptean in the CRM this morning and saw your name on
it. I didn't know that before today, so I figured I'd come to you right
away instead of letting you find out some other way.

Here's where it stands. I got to Clifford D'Souza, their Treasury
Director, on LinkedIn, and we've got a call Tuesday October 13 at 10:00
ET. He's bringing his Treasury Manager, Madhusudan N, who's in Bengaluru
and staying on until 7:30 his evening for it.

I can see this account's had work on it before I came near it, and I'm
not trying to plant a flag on something you've been building.

What I'd really like is for you to be on the call with me. Aptean isn't a
soft one. They're running 150 bank accounts across 100 entities and 10
banks, they've already done cash pooling and account rationalization, and
the direction they talk about publicly is fewer banking relationships,
not more. I'd rather have you in the room than take that solo.

Two things I want to run by you.

First, how do you want to handle the account? It's yours in the system
and I'll go with whatever works for you.

Second, is there anyone over there you're already talking to? I'd rather
we show up as one company than two guys arriving separately.

I've put together a pretty detailed prep note on the company and both
people. Happy to send it over whenever you want it.

Marcel

*If the recipient is notably senior, "two guys" becomes "two people".
Everything else holds.*

## The situation

Aptean is claimed in Monex's CRM by **Jason Roof**. Marcel discovered
this on 2026-09-23 while going to register the account after booking the
Oct 13 meeting with Clifford D'Souza.

## What the CRM record actually shows

**Jason Roof's recent activity:**
- Email to **Clifford D'Souza**, 2026-07-31, marked **Unopened**
- Task from 2026-07-20, **still overdue**: "Contact Aptean (had meeting
  w/ Kantox) / John Meade"
- BDR calling sequence to Hiroshi Tanaka, 16 steps, June 2026, no answer
  at any step

**Legacy history**, showing how long this account has circled without
landing: cold calls June 2024 to Tehmina Irfan, Susan Ricker in treasury
and Rahul Radhakrishnan, all unanswered. Notes from 2019 naming Kevin
McAdams as CFO and Brad DeBold as SVP Corporate Development, with the
account passing between Helen Given, Alexander Stolz and others.

**The fact worth keeping for the desk, not for the email:** Jason's
direct email to Clifford was never opened. Marcel's LinkedIn message
reached the same person and drew a reply in three hours, six weeks later.

## How Marcel is playing it

Going to Jason first, before any manager, and before Jason could learn it
from the CRM or from Clifford. That is the move that keeps this a
courtesy rather than a conflict.

**Deliberately left out of the email:**

- **That Jason never got a meeting.** True, and the reason Marcel feels
  entitled to a share, but in writing it reads as *I did what you could
  not*. The CRM says it without Marcel pointing.
- **Any ranking of himself below Jason.** An early draft said "you know
  more FX than I do" and considered mentioning that English is not
  Marcel's first language. Both hand a rival a reason to take the lead
  and cost standing that will be wanted later. Credit what Jason brings
  without grading yourself.
- **A pre-offered account split.** Account ownership and comp have rules
  Marcel may not be able to commit unilaterally. "How do you want to
  handle it" is stronger and often produces a better answer than
  conceding half up front.
- **The date of the LinkedIn outreach**, at Marcel's decision on
  2026-09-23. The honesty is carried by "I had not realised before
  today", which is true. A courtesy note does not owe a colleague a
  timeline.

**The separate obligation:** the CRM is a record, not a narrative. Log
the LinkedIn activity accurately when registering. Omitting a detail from
an email and misdating a regulated firm's activity log are different
orders of thing.

If Jason does see the date, it is not damaging. Prospecting first and
registering on traction is ordinary practice.

## Held back for the conversation, not the email

**Offering Jason the lead on the call.** The strongest team player signal
available, more likely to be declined or halved than accepted, and worth
more said out loud than written down. If he does take it, Marcel is still
in the room on an account he sourced.

**The prep note.** Offered in the email, sent only once Jason replies.

## If it goes quiet

Two business days, then one line: *"Following up on the below, mainly so
we are not both in front of them separately."*

If still quiet, go to a manager with a **question, not a complaint**:
*"Jason owns Aptean, I have a meeting booked, how would you like us to
handle it?"* That is a process question and it is very hard to answer
badly.

## What it settles

**The courtesy first play worked.** No flag planted, no conflict, no
manager needed. "I'll gladly collaborate" is an acceptance of a shared
account, offered without being asked for a split. The contingency plan
below, two business days then a nudge then a process question to a
manager, is now dead and can be ignored.

## What it opens, which is larger

**Jason was at Kantox and Aptean was his prospect there.** The CRM task
"Contact Aptean (had meeting w/ Kantox) / John Meade" was written from
his own history, not as an observation about a competitor. John Meade is
most likely the Aptean side contact from that pursuit.

This makes the Friday call worth far more than a scheduling courtesy.
The prep note's biggest open risk was walking into 13 October without
knowing whether Aptean is already inside BNP via Kantox. **Jason can
probably just tell him.**

What to get from Jason on Friday, in this order:

1. **Did Aptean buy Kantox, and if so when and how much of it.** The
   single fact that decides which half of the prepared call happens.
2. **Who actually decided.** Clifford, Madhu, or someone above them.
3. **What the objection was**, if they did not buy.
4. **Who John Meade is** and whether he is still there.
5. **What their setup looked like from the inside**, which is worth more
   than anything reconstructed from LinkedIn.

## Still held for the call, not for email

**Offering Jason the lead on the 13th.** Unchanged. Said out loud on
Friday, not written down. More likely to be declined or halved than
accepted, and worth the most as a spoken offer.

**The account split.** Jason did not raise it and neither should the
reply. "How do you want to handle it" was already asked in the first
email; let him answer it on Friday in his own words.

## The prep note went with the reply

Sent on Jason's reply, as planned. One line was softened first: a
sentence describing how long Monex had been "circling this account
without landing it" now reads as how long the account has been worked
and by how many people. True either way, and the first version is a barb
aimed at the man who owns it.


## The reply Marcel sent, 2026-09-24

> Jason, I appreciate that. Thank you.
>
> Friday works. Pick whatever time suits you once you are back and I will
> work around it. I am on Central.
>
> I am sending the prep note now so you have it before we talk: the
> company, Clifford, and Madhusudan N, their Treasury Manager in
> Bengaluru who is joining the call.
>
> The Kantox history is the part I most want to hear. I had read that CRM
> task as Aptean having taken a meeting with Kantox, not as you having
> carried them yourself. Knowing first hand how they buy changes how we
> run the call on the 13th.
>
> Safe travels.
>
> Marcel

**A first draft opened "Jason, that is good of you."** Marcel flagged
that he did not understand the phrase, which is the right test: he
should not send a line he cannot say out loud, because Jason may quote
it back on the call. It also leans British, against the standing
American English rule. Replaced with "I appreciate that." Same edit for
"fit around it" to "work around it" and "changes the shape of the call"
to "changes how we run the call".

**Note the "we" in how we run the call.** It assumes the collaboration
Jason just offered without negotiating anything, and it keeps Marcel in
the room by default rather than by permission.

### Rule for drafting in Marcel's voice

Plain American English, and nothing he would not say out loud. Idioms
that read well but do not survive being spoken back to him are a
liability, not polish. When in doubt, use the shorter and more literal
phrase.
