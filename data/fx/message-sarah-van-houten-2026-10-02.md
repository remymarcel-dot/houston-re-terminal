## The angle: a keto bill of materials is quietly imported

This is the part worth getting right, because the obvious note to a snack brand
is generic and would waste her.

A conventional cereal is corn, wheat, oats and sugar, and almost all of that is
domestic. **Catalina Crunch is keto**, which means the ingredient deck is
different in kind: pea protein, chicory root fiber, monk fruit, allulose. Those
are European and Asian supply chains. Pea protein out of France and Canada,
chicory fiber largely Belgian and Dutch, allulose and monk fruit out of Asia.

So the claim is specific and checkable: **a keto brand's bill of materials is
more imported than a conventional one of the same size.** She will know
immediately whether that is right, which is what makes it worth saying.

## What the note does not assume

It does not claim she carries the exposure. A brand this size often buys through
US ingredient distributors, in which case the currency risk sits in someone
else's margin and reaches her as price rather than as FX.

**So the note names both branches and asks which one Catalina is.** Same handling
as Jamie LaChapelle and James Schofield, and for the same reason: being wrong
about a CFO's own supply chain is worse than being vague.

Her About lists **Cash Management** among her skills, so she will follow the
argument without any of it being explained.
