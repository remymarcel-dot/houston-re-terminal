## Why now: the reason to call is two months old and it is his desk

**On 1 August 2026 DXP acquired Mequipco Ltd. of Calgary, Alberta.** Three locations
across Western Canada, roughly **CAD $9.9 million** in trailing sales and CAD $2.4
million EBITDA, funded with cash off the balance sheet plus DXP stock. It is the
beachhead for DXP Water in Canada and puts the platform into Alberta, British
Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba.

**Why a Treasurer cares, and these are the four things to have ready:**

1. A **new CAD functional currency subsidiary** lands on the consolidated balance
   sheet, so there is translation exposure that did not exist in July.
2. A **recurring CAD payables and receivables flow** out of Calgary, brand new.
3. **Intercompany funding** into Canada, which is a repeated conversion, not a
   one time event.
4. The purchase price itself settled partly in cash across the border.

**This is DXP's fifth acquisition of 2026.** PREMIERflow and Mid Atlantic Storage
Systems in January, Ambiente H2O in February (Sheridan, Colorado, so domestic, no
FX), General Repair Service in June (Minnesota, domestic), Mequipco in August. A
treasurer absorbing five integrations in nine months is busy, which cuts both ways:
harder to reach, and more likely to have an unsolved problem.

## The opening, roughly 20 seconds

> Russell, Marcel Remy at Monex USA in The Woodlands. I will be quick. I saw DXP
> picked up Mequipco in Calgary over the summer. I spent years as a CFO before this
> and the part I always underestimated was how much recurring Canadian dollar flow
> a single acquisition creates. I wanted to ask how you are handling that one, and
> if it is already solved I will say so and get off the phone.

**Why this shape:** it names the specific deal, it offers the peer credential rather
than a pitch, it asks a question instead of claiming a benefit, and it gives him a
clean exit. The exit is the part that keeps people on the line.

## Three questions worth asking, in order

1. Is the Calgary flow being converted by the bank at the time of payment, or is
   there a view on the rate before it goes?
2. Is Mequipco's own supplier base paying in CAD or USD? (A Canadian rep firm often
   buys from US manufacturers, which means **DXP may now own a company with the
   opposite exposure to the parent**. That is the interesting case.)
3. Who actually executes it, him or someone in the business?

## What NOT to say

- **Do not mention Ron Queen.** His name was used in the April invitation, it did
  not land, and invoking a referral twice in six months reads as name dropping.
  **If Marcel wants the referral, the right move is asking Ron for a real
  introduction, not naming him on a cold call.**
- **Do not lead with the LinkedIn invitation.** If it comes up, acknowledge it
  plainly and move on. Never imply a relationship that does not exist.
- **No rate quote, no product, no spread talk.** A treasurer at a NASDAQ company
  has a bank syndicate and will not be moved on a first call by pricing.
- **Do not say "save money."** Say what the exposure is and ask how it is handled.
