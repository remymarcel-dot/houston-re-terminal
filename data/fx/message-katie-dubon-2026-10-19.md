## Why each country line is true, for Marcel's own confidence

**Guatemala.** The quetzal is managed by Banguat inside a narrow band and has been
unusually stable for years. Remittances run at a very large share of GDP and are the
dominant source of dollar inflow, which is what holds it steady. Low volatility is the
point: when the rate does not move, every cent of cost is the spread, and spreads on
small corridors are the widest there are.

**Honduras.** The lempira runs as a managed crawl against the dollar, so depreciation
arrives in small controlled steps rather than jumps. **The operationally important
point is dollar availability**, which has been genuinely constrained at times, meaning
the problem is sometimes getting dollars at all rather than their price. A CFO who has
had a Honduran payment sit will recognize that instantly, and almost nobody leads with
it.

**Costa Rica.** The colón trades on a managed float with central bank intervention and
**appreciated sharply over 2023 and 2024**, which was a serious problem for Costa Rican
exporters and a widely discussed one. That direction matters for Bravo: a stronger colón
raises the dollar cost of anything priced locally.

**Colombia.** The peso floats freely and is the most volatile of the four by a wide
margin, driven by oil, US rates and domestic politics, with multi hundred peso swings
inside single years. Any single budget assumption here carries real risk.
