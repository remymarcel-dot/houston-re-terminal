## The one line that decides everything

His own text names **inventory financing** among what he structures.

That matters more than anything else on the profile, because it answers the
question that otherwise makes a 3PL a bad prospect:

- **A 3PL that only moves its clients' goods has no currency exposure of its
  own.** The brands own the inventory, the brands pay the factories, and the
  logistics company is paid in dollars for a service.
- **A company that finances inventory is buying it.** And apparel inventory is
  bought where apparel is made.

So the InMail does not assert that Quetico has exposure. **It asks whether the
financing means Quetico pays the factories**, which is the one fact that decides
whether there is a conversation at all. Getting that wrong in a cold InMail to
someone who runs a $300M operation would end it immediately.

## Why the note opens by admitting it is cold

He can see there are no mutual connections as easily as we can. Pretending
otherwise, or manufacturing a warm tone, is the thing that makes an InMail feel
like a blast. Naming it and then being short is the opposite.

## Notes

**The subject line quotes his profile.** It is specific enough that he knows
before opening it that someone read past the headline, which is the only thing
that gets a cold InMail opened at his level.

**No flattery about the career arc.** Sales Associate to EVP in six years is
genuinely impressive and tempting to mention, but praise from a stranger selling
something reads as technique. The research shows through the inventory financing
observation instead, which is more convincing because it is useful rather than
nice.

**Nothing about Ethos Brands or RVCA.** It is the more glamorous half of his
profile and it would dilute a note whose whole strength is one narrow question.
If he replies, RVCA's international wholesale is the obvious second thread.

**Canada appears only in the setup.** Canadian operations mean real CAD payroll
and lease exposure, but leading with it would be weaker than the financing point
and the message can only carry one argument.

## If he replies

If he says Quetico pays factories in local currency, that is a recurring,
sizeable, forecastable book and the conversation is immediate. If he says always
dollars, say so plainly and ask instead about the Canadian operation, which is
the exposure that does not depend on the financing answer.
