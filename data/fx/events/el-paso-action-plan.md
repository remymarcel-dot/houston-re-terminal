# El Paso summit: the plan, built around Oct 7

Event: Manufacturing and Supply Chain Nearshoring Summit, El Paso,
**Oct 21 to 22 2026**. Monex USA has **Stand 40**.
**B2B matchmaking opens roughly Oct 7**, two weeks before.

That date is what the plan hangs on. A meeting request from a name someone
already recognises converts far better than one from a stranger, so the
next two and a half weeks are for building recognition, not for pitching.

## Timeline

| when | what | why |
|---|---|---|
| **Sep 22 to Oct 6** | Connection requests to target seats at priority exhibitors. No pitch, no meeting ask. | They must recognise the name before the meeting request lands |
| **~Oct 7** | Matchmaking opens. Request meetings with the priority list on day one. | Slots go to whoever moves first |
| **Oct 8 to Oct 20** | Follow up with anyone who accepted a connection but not a meeting. Booth invitation, not a pitch. | Second route to the same people |
| **Oct 21 to 22** | Work the floor from the booth prep list. | |

At 15 connection requests a day the capacity is far more than the target
list needs. Volume is not the constraint here; timing is.

## Why connect now rather than pitch now

A cold pitch three weeks before an event asks for a decision they have no
reason to make yet. A connection request costs them nothing, and it means
that when the matchmaking request arrives on Oct 7 it comes from a face
they have seen. That is the entire purpose of the pre-event window.

The note on the connection request, if any, should mention the summit and
nothing else. No FX, no rates, no savings table. Save all of it for the
meeting.

## Sourcing constraint, stated plainly

Apollo returns masked surnames and no LinkedIn URLs, and HeyReach needs a
profile URL to add anyone to a campaign. So this cannot be run as an
automated campaign against strangers. The workable route is Marcel or the
agent finding each person on LinkedIn from first name, title and company,
then sending the request.

That is slower per person, but the target list is twenty to thirty
companies, not hundreds, and the quality of the meeting matters more than
the count.
