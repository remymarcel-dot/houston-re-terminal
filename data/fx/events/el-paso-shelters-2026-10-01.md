## What stand 84 actually is

The directory reads "SACSA AND MAQUILA PARTNERS", which I had been treating as
one shelter with a clumsy name. It is two companies co-exhibiting, and they do
different things:

- **SACSA** is a customs broker, an *agencia aduanal*, not a shelter at all.
- **MaquilaPartner** is the shelter.

Broker plus shelter is the natural bundle you sell to a company entering Mexico
for the first time: one party moves the goods across, the other holds the IMMEX
and employs the people. So the 80/81/84/88 block is a **trade services cluster**,
not four rivals competing for the same customer. That matters for how you work
the aisle. You can talk to all of them in one pass without any of them feeling
shopped.

## 1. Agencia Aduanal SACSA, the one worth a matchmaking slot

**El Paso, Texas. ZoomInfo id 547110367. www.aasacsa.com. 77 employees,
revenue around $4.5M.** Twenty five years old. Air, sea and land cargo, plus
bonded warehousing between Mexico and the US.

This is the only one of the four with a US entity, a named finance seat and a
live data record all at once. It is also the one I nearly discarded, because a
customs broker was not what I went looking for.

**Why a broker is a better prospect than the shelter was.** Two revenue lines,
not one:

1. **Direct.** Brokers disburse on behalf of clients constantly, duties, taxes,
   carrier payments, warehouse fees, much of it to Mexican counterparties. That
   is real volume moving through someone's FX spread today.
2. **Referral.** A broker with 77 people sits on top of dozens of importers.
   One relationship here can see more deal flow than one shelter client ever
   would.

Lead with the first and let the second surface on its own. Walking in asking to
be referred is how you get politely handled.
