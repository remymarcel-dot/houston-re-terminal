**Marcel guessed the exposure was reais in, euros out. Alan said no — and then described a
larger exposure instead.** Two legs, in opposite directions:

1. **EUR receivables converted to USD** in the Midland entity. He is a euro *seller*. The

**A construction schedule is a known future requirement in reais**, which is a completely
different problem from converting at spot on each draw. The risk is not the average rate
across the build; it is that **the budget was set at one rate and the draws happen at
another** — the same mechanism as Gloria Mancilla's Temple plant and Rodolfo Vela's
carve-out, and the reason builds come in over budget without anyone calling it FX.

**And there is a genuinely favourable fact here that most people funding Brazilian capex
find out late:** Brazilian interest rates sit well above US rates, so the forward market
tends to price reais weaker further out. **That works in favour of someone buying reais with
dollars rather than against them.** It is structural, not a forecast, and it is the kind of
thing a Midland pipe executive building in Brazil has no reason to know.
