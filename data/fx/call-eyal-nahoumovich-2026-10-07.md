## The real content, and the best question on the sheet

Five sourcing countries is five different situations, not one. The useful structure:

- **Chile and Peru.** USD invoicing is the norm and the grower absorbs the
  conversion. **That does not mean it is free.** The grower prices his own currency
  risk into the USD number, so Gaia pays for it without ever seeing a rate.
- **India.** Exporters almost always invoice in dollars; the rupee is managed. Likely
  the quietest of the five.
- **China.** This is where it gets real. Onshore and offshore renminbi do not price
  identically, and a supplier who wants RMB rather than dollars is a different
  conversation from one who does not.
- **MOROCCO IS THE BEST QUESTION, AND IT IS NON OBVIOUS.** The dirham is pegged to a
  basket weighted toward the euro, and Morocco's trade is Europe facing. **So a
  Moroccan supplier may well invoice in euros rather than dollars.** If that is true,
  Gaia has a euro exposure that nobody in the business has ever called a euro
  exposure. Ask it plainly:

> When you buy out of Morocco, are you paying that in dollars or in euros?

**That one question is worth the call.** It is specific, it is checkable in seconds
by him, and whichever way he answers it tells us what to do next. Present it as a
question, never as a claim about his business.

## Size: handle with care

**ZoomInfo puts Gaia Produce revenue at $572,000, and that number is almost certainly
wrong.** An importer running programs in Chile, Peru, China, India and Morocco is not
a half million dollar business. ZoomInfo routinely understates small private
importers.

**But it has not been disproven either.** So the call's quiet second job is sizing,
and it can be done without asking a rude question. Container counts, how many
programs run at once, whether he buys on his own book or brokers for others, all of
that sizes him naturally in conversation. His own company description says importer,
broker, distributor AND marketer, which are four different balance sheets.

**If he turns out to be genuinely small, say so in the log and let the next decision
be made on it.** Do not keep spending calls on a half million dollar account.

## What NOT to say

- **No reproach about the unanswered message.** One light acknowledgment, then move
  on. People who ignore a message and then get scolded for it do not become clients.
- **Do not re-explain the five country argument.** He read it. Repeating it tells him
  Marcel does not remember what he sent.
- **No rate quote and no product.** The call is a question, not a pitch.
- **Do not claim to know how Gaia pays anyone.** Every point above is a hypothesis
  about the countries, not a finding about his company.
