## How the waves are ordered

Not by count and not by company size. By two things: whether the FX
story is one nobody else is telling them, and whether the person who
feels the pain is reachable in a company that small.

## Wave 1: travel operators

**Why first.** Every FX shop in Texas calls Nogales produce and Laredo
logistics. Almost nobody calls a tour operator, and the operators have
the cleanest exposure in the whole book: they publish a price in dollars
a year ahead and pay the lodge, ship or ground operator in that
country's currency when the trip actually runs. The margin is set at
brochure time and spent twelve months later at whatever rate shows up.
That is a forward contract described in plain English, and it is their
own business described back to them.

**The screen inside the segment matters more than the segment.** Code 23
holds two different businesses. Retail and host agencies earn commission
on suppliers who bill them in dollars, so they carry no FX at all. Cut:
Brownell Travel, McCabe World Travel, Travelwise, avenuetwotravel, Five
Star Alliance, Jetset World Travel, Unique Travel of Palm Beach, Milk +
Honey Travels, My Travel Wizards, Beyond the Moon Travel, BOOST
Journeys.

Outbound operators carry the payable themselves. Those are the targets.

Also cut from code 23: African Travel Inc, owned by The Travel
Corporation, so treasury is captive at the parent. US Merchants and
Foremost Fresh Direct are misfiled and belong in other segments. Escape
Adventures runs bike tours in US national parks, so the FX thesis does
not hold.

## Wave 2: Nogales produce, then the Hispanic food importers

**Why second and not first: timing, which is the only argument that
beats differentiation.** Mexican winter vegetable crossings ramp from
late October through March, so Nogales importers set season pricing and
grower terms in October. A forward conversation in early October lands
on a decision they are actually making. In February it lands on a
decision they already made.

Seven Nogales accounts: Malena Produce, Big Chuy Distributors, Ciruli
Brothers, Tricar Sales, Kaliroy Fresh, Wilson Produce, Splendid by
Porvenir.

The code 15 food importers follow immediately behind and are the
largest single segment in the book. Marcel's Spanish is a real edge
here and most are family owned, so the controller or the owner is one
seat away.

Cut on country before sourcing starts: Dulces Mara (.com.mx), Induveca
(Dominican Republic), WE Soda (United Kingdom), House of Tara (Nigeria),
Welldex (Mexico). CopanTrade is Canadian and stays in, since Monex
onboards Canadian companies. Barcel is the US arm of Grupo Bimbo, so
treasury is captive at the parent. Grupo Palco is Mexican and already
live as a booth meeting at Stand 40, so it is not a campaign target.

## Wave 3: stone and surfaces

Six accounts, and the most underserved thing in the book. Slab
importers buy from Brazil, Italy, Turkey and India, which means real
BRL, EUR, TRY and INR exposure on containers ordered months ahead.
Pacific Shore Stones, Marble Unlimited, Omni Surfaces, Prestige Marble
and Designs, Amanzi Marble and Granite, Ferretti Granite and Marble,
Unlimited Marble and Granite, Kol Marble Granite.

## Wave 4: floral importers

Twelve Miami and California accounts. Thinner than it looks, because
Colombian and Ecuadorian farms mostly invoice in dollars. The real
opening is payment cost and speed rather than hedging, which is a weaker
opener and belongs behind the three waves above.

## Wave 5: cross border logistics

Eighteen accounts, mostly Laredo. They pay Mexican carriers in pesos, so
the exposure is genuine, but this is the most heavily worked corridor in
the market and it overlaps with plays already running. Behind the others
on differentiation, not on merit.

## What this book is worth

Ninety seven accounts survive the screen out of 170. None of them have
a contact attached in the CRM, which is why the book has sat still since
2018: it is a list of company names, not a list of people. Every wave
here costs one sourcing pass to turn a row into a seat, and Wave 1 is
already done.

## Wave 2 build state

Wave 2 did not survive contact with the data in the shape it was
planned, and the reason is worth keeping.

**The Nogales seven yield four seats, not seven.** Big Chuy Distributors
has an administrator and an employee on LinkedIn and nothing else.
Kaliroy Fresh has no one at all. Splendid by Porvenir has a sales
coordinator. These are small family houses that do not maintain a
LinkedIn presence, so for those three the CRM row is a phone and email
play, not a campaign target. Sourcing them again through LinkedIn will
keep returning the same nothing.

**The Hispanic food segment is mostly US manufacturers, not importers.**
Cacique, V&V Supremo, Nuestro Queso and Winona Foods all have good
LinkedIn coverage and several finance seats each, and all four are US
manufacturers of Hispanic style food rather than importers of it. The
peso payables thesis does not hold for them, so coverage is not the same
thing as fit. Cacique additionally returns four different current CFO
records, which is stale data and would need verifying before anyone is
approached there.

So Wave 2 is the thesis, not the segment: US companies that pay Mexican
suppliers, wherever they sit in the book.
