## Cut for having no FX thesis

Not cut for being small or unreachable. Cut because there is nothing
true to say to them about currency.

**The Pavement Group, Mark Kuczinski, CFO.** Wexford PA, 47 people,
founded 2018. Asphalt and concrete repair for US facilities through a
nationwide contractor network. No import, no export, no foreign
supplier. Writing to him about currency exposure would be inventing a
premise, and it would read as a list.

**Miller Insulation, Corey Hager, CFO.** Bismarck ND, 350 people, $157M.
Industrial insulation and scaffolding for refineries, ethanol plants and
Bakken oilfield work. Employee owned. Entirely domestic.

These two are the reason the batch was verified rather than assumed. By
the logic Marcel applied to Barcel, a message to a first degree
connection costs nothing scarce, so the bar is low. But it is not zero:
a currency question to a pavement contractor spends credibility for
nothing.

## The four, and why each one holds

### Randy Scheid, CFO, Plastic Design & Manufacturing
Manhattan MT, 38 people. **The only one with documented foreign
sourcing:** the company record carries "offshore tooling", "global
sourcing" and "domestic and offshore production" explicitly. CFO since
November 2024, President and CFO before that.

**He is a second touch, not a first.** The collision check found an
existing thread: one message on 2026-08-27, unanswered, which read
*"I work with finance teams on international payments and FX. Coming
across manufacturers dealing with imported materials and overseas
suppliers fairly often, so it seemed worth connecting here."* Generic,
no question, and it went nowhere. He was not in `pipeline.json`, only in
the inbox, the same gap that nearly cost F&S Fresh Foods.

So his opener names the weak first message and replaces it with the
offshore tooling point. Same pattern as the Jenna Bussard follow up:
once, with something concrete, rather than another check in.

### Dale Sexton, CFO, AFCO Industries
Alexandria LA, 320 people, $75.5M. Aluminum, fiberglass and PVC
extrusion for building products.

**His LinkedIn headline is stale** and still reads Southland Steel
Fabricators. Apollo's employment history shows Southland ended January
2026 and AFCO began the same month, so AFCO is current and he is nine
months in. Career runs Kinder Morgan, an M&A analyst seat at Lumen, VP
Finance at PosiGen, then four CFO seats at once across the Southland
group.

The FX thesis here is **assumed, not documented**, so the opener asks
rather than asserts. The real hook is the timing: nine months into a
seat with an M&A background is when inherited contracts get opened.

### Kate Clark, CFO, Clark Bros
Fresno CA, 110 people, $65.4M, founded 1958. Earthwork and grading, water
and wastewater treatment, and renewables EPC covering wind, solar,
battery and high voltage. CFO since 2019, MBA and CCIFP.

Thesis also **asked rather than asserted**: water treatment and high
voltage equipment often comes from Europe and Asia. The line that earns
her attention is the construction finance one, that on a fixed price
contract the currency moves between the bid and the purchase order
without anyone repricing the job. A CCIFP will recognize that
immediately.

### Matt Wharton, CFO, PURIS
Minneapolis, 220 people, $80M. Pea protein and plant based ingredients.
CFO since September 2023.

**His opener says out loud that the import thesis does not apply.** PURIS
sells on North American growing and local sourcing, so there is no
payable story, and pretending otherwise would be caught instantly. It
pivots to the receivable side: pea protein is a global market, the
European competition prices in euros, and export volume carries currency
on the receivable. Then asks whether export is a big enough share to
matter.
